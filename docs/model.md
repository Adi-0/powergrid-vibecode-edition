# The model

What is modelled, with which equations, and where every parameter comes from.

## Source families

Parameter values are anchored to these families. No page numbers or table numbers are
cited (the brief forbids fabricating them); where a value is not confidently canonical
it is marked **estimate** in its data file and here.

- **Power system analysis texts** — Glover, Overbye & Sarma, *Power System Analysis
  and Design*; Grainger & Stevenson, *Power System Analysis*. (Per-unit, Ybus,
  Newton–Raphson, symmetrical components, fault analysis, line constants.)
- **Distribution** — Kersting, *Distribution System Modeling and Analysis*. (Carson's
  equations, Kron reduction, backward/forward sweep, regulators, the IEEE 13-node feeder.)
- **Stability** — Kundur, *Power System Stability and Control*. (Swing equation,
  inertia constants, governor droop, equal-area criterion, machine parameters.)
- **Protection** — Blackburn & Domin, *Protective Relaying: Principles and Applications*.
- **Standards** — ANSI C84.1 (service voltage ranges), IEEE C37.2 (device function
  numbers), IEEE C37.112 (inverse-time characteristic equations), IEEE 738 (conductor
  ampacity), IEEE 80 (substation grounding), IEEE 1366 (reliability indices),
  IEEE C57.12.00 (transformer terminal markings and vector groups).

Every parameter of the synthetic network, conductor, construction, technology,
plant and load is tabulated in **[model-tables.md](model-tables.md)**, generated
directly from `src/data/` so it cannot drift from the model.

## Per-unit system

S_base = 100 MVA for the whole system. Each bus's voltage base is its nominal
line-to-line kV. Z_base = V_base²/S_base, I_base = S_base/(√3·V_base). Transformers
use nominal ratios equal to the bus bases (off-nominal tap τ = 1), so per-unit
impedances pass through them unchanged — which is why the field uses per unit.

## Transmission power flow (`src/physics/pf/`)

- **Ybus** (MATPOWER convention): branch π model with series y_s = 1/(R + jX), half the
  charging jB/2 at each end, complex tap τ∠θ at the from end.
- **Newton–Raphson, polar**, unknowns θ (all but the reference) and |V| (PQ buses),
  plus λ for distributed slack. Mismatches ΔP_i = P_i(V,θ) − P_sched,i,
  ΔQ_i = Q_i(V,θ) − Q_sched,i with P_i = |V_i|Σ|V_k|(G_ik cos θ_ik + B_ik sin θ_ik),
  Q_i = |V_i|Σ|V_k|(G_ik sin θ_ik − B_ik cos θ_ik). Converged to 1e-9 pu.
- **Starting point**: warm start from the previous interval, else DC angles.
- **Limits**: P limits pin units and re-share λ; Q limits switch PV→PQ bus by bus.
- **Islands** are found first; an island with load and no source is dark (no
  operating point), never a low-voltage "solution".
- **Validation**: IEEE 14-bus, MATPOWER case30 and the original IEEE 30-bus, against
  pandapower 3.5.5 (fixtures in `test/fixtures/`, regenerated by `tools/ref/ieee_pf.py`):
  |V| to 1e-6 pu, angles to 1e-4°, generator and branch-end flows to 1e-4 MW/MVAr.

## Line constants (`src/physics/lineconstants.ts`)

Modified Carson's equations (Kersting) with earth resistivity 100 Ω·m at 60 Hz,
Kron reduction of neutrals and shield wires, potential coefficients with images for
capacitance. Bundles use equivalent GMR/radius. Validated against the IEEE 13-node
configurations 601–605 (Z and B, every element, to the published four decimals).
Transmission lines use the transposed-line averages for sequence values and the
**exact π** for whole-line parameters: Z′ = Z_c sinh(γℓ), Y′/2 = tanh(γℓ/2)/Z_c.

## The synthetic California network (`src/data/ca/`)

Named after real places; **not a replica**. 57 sites (three are interties in Oregon,
Nevada and Arizona), 78 buses, 500 / 230 / 115 / 60 kV. Structure: the COI and
COTP bring Northwest power to the Sacramento Valley; the 500 kV backbone runs through
Tesla, Los Banos (Path 15), Gates and Midway, over the Tehachapis (Path 26) to
Vincent, then east to Lugo/Eldorado and south-east to Devers, Colorado River and Palo
Verde; San Diego is fed from Imperial Valley (Southwest Powerlink / Sunrise) and from
the north over Path 44. Load centres: Bay Area, Sacramento, Fresno, Bakersfield, Los
Angeles Basin, Inland Empire, San Diego. Generation: Diablo Canyon (nuclear), Sierra
and Shasta hydro, Helms pumped storage, The Geysers and Salton Sea (geothermal), wind
at Solano, Altamont, Tehachapi and San Gorgonio, solar in the Central Valley, Carrizo,
Antelope Valley, the desert and Imperial Valley, coastal and basin gas, batteries,
synchronous condensers. Reference peak demand 50.7 GW (the modelled day peaks at
93 % of that); 17 GW of rooftop solar netted from it.

Security: every interval converges within normal ratings; at the evening peak ≥ 95 %
of single outages stay within emergency ratings; tight corridors are listed in
`src/data/ca/network.ts` and decision 0009.

## Dispatch and the quasi-static day (`src/model/`)

See decision 0008. Costs: variable cost = heat rate × (fuel price + CO₂ price ×
emission factor) + variable O&M; interties in price blocks. Round-trip efficiency:
batteries 86 %, pumped storage 76 % (estimates).

## Resources (`src/physics/solar.ts`, `src/data/ca/profiles.ts`)

NOAA solar position; Meinel clear-sky beam irradiance (DNI = 1353·0.7^(AM^0.678)),
diffuse = 12 % of beam (estimate); single-axis trackers (±60°) or fixed tilt; NOCT cell
temperature with γ_T = −0.37 %/°C; DC/AC 1.3 (utility) and 1.15 (rooftop, with a 0.8
orientation/shading diversity factor); inverter efficiency 98 %. Demand and wind are
hourly shapes of the CAISO kind (estimates).

(Distribution, protection, machines and dynamics sections follow in later phases.)
