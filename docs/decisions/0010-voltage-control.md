# 0010 — Voltage and reactive power

**Chosen.** Synchronous units regulate their bus voltage within reactive capability
(from rated power factor); PV→PQ switching is bus by bus with release when the
voltage recovers. Inverter plants run at unity power factor. Switched shunts: reactors
at 500 kV buses (sized to part of the line charging landing there) and capacitor banks
at 230/115 kV load buses (sized to the load's peak reactive demand), stepped by a
voltage-band controller *and* stepped in wherever a region's machines sit at their
reactive maximum — operators keep dynamic reactive reserve for contingencies, and
without that rule the south ran with ~500 MVAr of reserve and collapsed on common
outages. Synchronous condensers and SVCs where the real system has them for the same
reason (Talega, Miguel, Santiago, Potrero's converter, Metcalf, Sacramento, Laguna
Bell, Goleta). Reliability must-run units in the Bay Area, LA Basin, San Diego,
Sacramento and Humboldt (local capacity requirements).

**Rejected.** Letting inverter plants regulate voltage (modern ones can): it would hide
the reactive-power story the synchronous condensers tell.
